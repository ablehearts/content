## The Irony of the Church Next Door

**Imagine this:**

You can HEAR the Sunday sermons from your living room. The church is literally **separated from your home by a dirt road**—so close you could throw a stone and hit the wall.

For decades, YOUR home was the church's:
- 🥁 **Storage facility** (drums, tithe dishes, instruments kept safely in your house)
- 🍳 **Catering kitchen** (fed church builders for YEARS with your mother's traditional cooking pots)
- 💧 **Resource center** (water, firewood, labor—all from Mary's household)

**You were BORN into this faith.** Baptized Catholic as a baby. Your mother was **highly respected** in the village—calm, honest, caring for TWO disabled people in one household.

**Then the roles reversed.**

The church—or rather, the **congregants**—now want your land. Your ONLY asset. Your last refuge.

**Not to help you. To build housing for the priest.**

## Born Into Faith, Not Choice

### The Legacy of Colonization

Mary & Lydia didn't "choose" Catholicism. **They were born into it.**

When British colonizers arrived in Kenya, they didn't just take land—they **indoctrinated faith**. Villages were assigned churches. Children were baptized. Sunday attendance became social currency.

**Mary's family?** Roman Catholic by colonial decree, not personal conviction.

But they participated. Mary's mother was **devout**:
- Attended every Sunday
- Stored church property safely
- Fed builders during the decades-long church construction
- Raised her daughters in the faith

**The church was family.** Until family became a liability.

## "The Priest is Busy": When Disability Meets Religious Indifference

### The Accessibility Barrier

**Mary and Lydia wanted to attend church.** They tried.

But here's what stopped them:
- ❌ **Inaccessible dirt road** (full of potholes, ditches, uneven surface)
  - Mary can't manage with old crutches and specialized shoes
  - Lydia can't take ANY non-flat route (her disability prevents walking on uneven terrain)
  - No wheelchair access whatsoever
- ❌ **No specialized seating** (they can't sit on wooden benches for 2+ hour services)
- ❌ **No congregant assistance** (who has time to help the "lesser beings"?)

**The church building?** Literally **30 meters away**. You can hear the choir. You can hear the priest's voice crackling through the cheap speakers.

**But those 30 meters might as well be 30 kilometers.**

**Mary can't navigate the rutted dirt road with her crutches.** One pothole could mean a fall, a broken bone, months of immobility.

**Lydia physically CANNOT walk on non-flat surfaces.** Her body doesn't allow it. She needs smooth, level ground—something the village has never provided.

**So they can't attend.**

---

### Multiple Requests, Zero Response

**Mary didn't give up.** She tried reaching the priest—**not once, not twice, but MULTIPLE times**:

1. **Request:** Can the priest visit for special prayers? (She can't get to the church.)
   - **Response:** "The priest is busy."

2. **Request:** Can someone help create an accessible pathway?
   - **Response:** Silence.

3. **Request:** Can the church provide a special seat for disabled congregants?
   - **Response:** "Why don't they just attend like everyone else?"

**The Irony?**

The priest's car drives **RIGHT PAST MARY'S HOUSE** every Sunday. The only road to the church goes through the junction where Mary lives.

**He could stop. He never did.**

---

### Even the Sweets Were Sinful

**Mary tried to HELP the church:**

During her kiosk days, she sold **sweets to children before Sunday service**. The kids would:
- Buy candy for 5-10 shillings
- Sit quietly in church (sugar-induced calm)
- Actually LISTEN to the sermon instead of fidgeting

**Parents loved it. Kids loved it.**

**Congregants?** They **bad-mouthed her**:
> She's corrupting the children.
> Making profit from God's day.
> The sweets are a distraction from worship.

**Translation:** How DARE a disabled woman run a successful business next to OUR church?

---

### The "Blessing Theft" Accusation

**Another absurdity:**

Villagers would bring **50-shilling notes** to church for tithe/offerings. But the collection dishes only had **small bills**—10 shillings, 20 shillings.

**Some congregants asked Mary's kiosk to break their 50-shilling notes into coins** (for easier giving).

**Church response?**
> You're STEALING the blessings by exchanging money at the kiosk instead of giving the full note to God!

**Let that sink in:**
- Giving 50 shillings in coins = Stealing blessings ❌
- Giving a 50-shilling note = Blessed ✅

**Even HELPING people give to the church was seen as exploitation.**

**Mary's Land (Junction Quadrant):**

2. ⛪ **Catholic Church** (directly across the dirt road)

6. 🛒 **Local Market** (end of the road)

**Translation:** This is **village prime real estate**. If local politics and economy had played out right, this location could've made Mary's family **prosperous**.

**Instead?** It painted a target on their backs.

**But the church already HAS facilities.** They just want MORE.
