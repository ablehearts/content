# Fashion Brands Must Create Shoes for Disabled People

## An Open Letter to Nike, Jordan, Adidas, Converse, Vans, Puma, and All Top Shoe Brands

**Dear Fashion Industry Leaders,**

You've mastered the art of creating shoes for athletes, celebrities, and streetwear enthusiasts. You've collaborated with rappers, basketball stars, and fashion icons. **But what about disabled people?** 👟♿

The disabled community represents **1.3 billion people globally**—that's 16% of the world's population (WHO). Yet, finding stylish, functional footwear remains a daily struggle for millions like Mary and Lydia in Kenya.

**It's time to change that.** And we have a proposal.

---

## 🦶 The Problem: Fashion Forgot Disabled People

### **The Current Reality**

When Mary needs shoes, she faces:
- ❌ **Standard shoes don't fit** orthopedic braces
- ❌ **Laces are impossible** to tie with limited hand mobility
- ❌ **No ankle support** for weak legs
- ❌ **Ugly medical shoes** that scream "disabled"
- ❌ **Expensive custom shoes** ($200-$600) she can't afford

When Lydia looks at fashion magazines, she sees:
- ✨ Beautiful Nikes, Jordans, Air Force 1s
- ✨ Trendy Adidas, Vans, Converse
- ✨ Stylish Pumas, New Balance, Reeboks

**But none of them work for her body.**

### **The Fashion Industry's Blind Spot**

Fashion brands spend billions on:
- 💰 Celebrity endorsements (Michael Jordan, Kanye West, Rihanna)
- 💰 Limited edition drops
- 💰 Sneaker culture marketing
- 💰 Athlete sponsorships

But **disabled people?** An afterthought. Maybe a token "accessible" line buried deep in the catalog, designed by non-disabled people who've never lived the reality.

---

## 👟 What Disabled People Actually Need

### **Functional Features**

Disability-inclusive shoes must have:

1. **✅ Easy Entry/Exit**
   - Magnetic closures (no laces!)
   - Wide openings for braces/casts
   - Velcro straps (adjustable, simple)
   - Zipper sides (quick access)

2. **✅ Orthopedic Support**
   - Reinforced ankle support
   - Custom insole compatibility
   - Extra depth for braces
   - Non-slip soles (wheelchairs, crutches)

3. **✅ Adaptive Sizing**
   - Asymmetric sizing (left ≠ right foot often)
   - Swelling accommodation
   - Brace-friendly width
   - Lightweight materials (less fatigue)

4. **✅ Durability**
   - Wheelchair footrests wear shoes fast
   - Crutch users drag feet
   - Need reinforced toes/heels
   - Long-lasting materials

### **Fashion Features (YES, Disabled People Want Style Too!)**

5. **✅ Beautiful Designs**
   - NOT "medical-looking"
   - Trendy colors and patterns
   - Streetwear-ready aesthetics
   - Logo branding (we want the Swoosh too!)

6. **✅ Representation**
   - Disabled models in ads
   - Real disabled ambassadors (not actors)
   - Authentic storytelling
   - Community co-design

## ❓ FAQs for Fashion Brands

### Q: Why should we partner with AbleHearts specifically?

**A:** Because we're **authentic, transparent, and connected to the real disability community**. We're not a big NGO with bureaucracy. We're directly supporting Mary & Lydia and can provide:
- Real-time feedback from disabled users
- Transparent impact reporting
- Genuine storytelling (no corporate sanitization)
- Direct connection to disabled communities in Africa

### Q: How much would this partnership cost?

**A:** Less than one celebrity endorsement deal. We're asking for:
- Product development investment (co-design process)
- Mary & Lydia ambassador fees (fair compensation, not exploitation!)
- Marketing budget (authentic campaigns)
- Charitable donation (portion of sales → AbleHearts)

**Total:** Comparable to a limited edition collab—but with **massive social impact**.

### Q: What if the shoes don't sell well?

**A:** The disability market is **underserved, not disinterested**. If you build it (and market it authentically), they WILL come. Plus:
- Crossover appeal (elderly, injured, convenience-seekers)
- Positive PR (even if sales are modest, brand reputation improves)
- Future-proof (population aging = more disabilities)

### Q: Can we just donate shoes instead of creating a line?

**A:** **NO.** Donation = pity. Product line = dignity. Disabled people want to **BUY** stylish shoes like everyone else. We don't want charity shoes; we want **equal access to fashion**.

### Q: How do we ensure authenticity (not "disability-washing")?

**A:**
- ✅ Hire disabled designers (not just consultants)
- ✅ Feature real disabled models (not actors playing disabled)
- ✅ Co-create with Mary, Lydia, and disability communities
- ✅ Long-term commitment (not a one-off PR stunt)
- ✅ Transparent communication (admit mistakes, iterate based on feedback)
