# Not Waiting for Miracles: Mary & Lydia's 25-Year Entrepreneurial Fight

**They are NOT sitting around waiting for handouts. They've been fighting for financial independence for over 25 years.**

Mary has tried **6 different businesses**. Lydia managed **2 businesses** despite immobility. Every single one collapsed—**not from laziness, but from systemic exploitation, predatory microfinance, medical crises, and a village economy designed to crush the vulnerable.**

This is their untold entrepreneurial story.

---

## ✂️ Late 1990s: Mary Learns Tailoring—Her First Dream

### The Breakthrough: Against All Odds

**Late 1990s (before the 21st century):**
- 🎓 **Mary enrolls in a tailoring course** at a local training institute  (Nyanchwa Technical and Vocational Training Centre)
- ♿ **Despite physical mobility challenges**, she masters the skills
- ✂️ **She gets a sewing machine**—her first business tool
- 🌟 **Hope is alive:** "I can work. I can earn. I can support my family."

### The Harsh Reality: Village Business = Exploitation

**Why the tailoring business failed:**

#### 1. **"We Raised You" Entitlement Culture**
- 👗 Villagers place orders but never pay
- 💬 **"We're neighbors, you can wait"**
- 💬 **"We helped your family, now you owe us"**
- 💬 **"You're disabled, you should be grateful we're 'supporting' your business"**

**Result:** Mary works for free, making clothes she never gets paid for.

#### 2. **"Who Pays a Disabled Woman?" Discrimination**
- 🚫 Customers refuse to pay full price
- 💬 **"You're slow because of your legs, why should I pay full price?"**
- 💬 **"Other tailors are faster, I'll pay you half"**
- ♿ **Disability = Discount in villagers' minds**

**Result:** Even when paid, it's below cost—unsustainable.

#### 3. **Cannot Follow Up for Payments**
- 🦽 **Mary's mobility is limited**—she can't chase debtors door-to-door
- 🏚️ Villagers know she can't "follow up" like able-bodied tailors
- 💸 **Debt accumulates, no enforcement possible**

**Result:** She's left with unpaid invoices, no legal recourse.

#### 4. **High Material Costs, Zero ROI**
- 🧵 Purchasing fabric and thread costs money upfront
- 📉 **Returns on Investment (ROI): ZERO**
- 💔 She's working at a loss, depleting savings

**Final Outcome:**
> **Tailoring career dies in early 2000s. But Mary never loses hope.**

## 🏪 2000s: Village Kiosk—Selling Fast-Moving Goods

### The Pivot: Small-Scale Retail

**Mary opens a village kiosk (small shop):**
- 🛒 **Sells fast-moving goods:** sugar, soap, flour, kerosene, sweets
- 🏡 **Typical village setting:** Small wooden structure, basic inventory
- 💪 **Mary manages it despite limited mobility**

### The Trap: Predatory Microfinance (KWFT Loan)

**Around 2010:**
- 💰 **Mary takes a loan from KWFT (Kenya Women Finance Trust)**—a microfinance institution
- 🎯 **Purpose:** Boost the kiosk business, buy more inventory
- 📈 **Hope:** Expand the business, increase profits

**The Loan Terms (Predatory):**
- 📊 **Interest rates: RIDICULOUS** (often 20-40% annual in Kenyan microfinance)
- ⏳ **Short repayment period** (weekly/monthly payments, no grace for emergencies)
- 📜 **Fine print villagers can't read**—Mary's mother signed papers she didn't fully understand
- 🚫 **No regulation:** Local government fails to protect vulnerable borrowers

### The Collapse: The Cow Is Repossessed

**What happened:**
- 📉 **Business profits can't cover loan repayments** (interest too high)
- 🩺 **Medical emergencies drain capital** (Mary's health issues, family needs)
- 💸 **KWFT demands repayment**
- 🐄 **KWFT repossesses the family's ONLY milking cow** instead of working out a payment plan

**The Cow = Family Lifeline:**
- 🥛 **Daily milk for nutrition**
- 💰 **Selling milk = income**
- 👨‍👩‍👧 **Symbol of family security**

### The Suicide Attempt: Breaking Point

**When the cow was taken:**
- 😭 **Mary's mother (now deceased, 2019 poisoning) attempts suicide**
- 🌊 **She goes to the river to drown herself**
- 🆘 **Villagers intercept and save her**
- 💔 **But the cow never comes back**

**This is the reality of predatory microfinance in Kenya:**
> Local government fails to regulate. Villagers sign papers they can't read. Microfinancers take EVERYTHING—even the family's survival assets.

**Final Outcome:**
> **Kiosk business collapses. Family deeper in poverty. But Mary keeps fighting.**

## 🍰 2010s: Baking, Detergents, Soaps—Hands-On Skills

### The Comeback: Community-Based Entrepreneurship

**Mary learns new skills through DETERMINATION:**

#### 1. **Baking Local Cakes**
- 🎂 **Weddings, birthdays, local events**
- 🍞 **Traditional Kenyan cakes, mandazis, chapatis**
- 👰 **Villagers order for celebrations**

#### 2. **Making Detergents**
- 🧪 **Mixing reagents and water**
- 🧴 **Selling locally-made laundry soap**
- ♻️ **Affordable for villagers (cheaper than store-bought)**

#### 3. **Making Bar Soaps**
- 🧼 **Handmade soaps for washing**
- 🌿 **Using local ingredients**

### The Vision: Empowering Other Disabled People

**Mary's dream:**
- 🤝 **Recruit other disabled people in the village**
- 📚 **Teach them these skills**
- 💪 **Create a cooperative of disabled entrepreneurs**
- 🌍 **Achieve financial independence together**

**Why this made sense:**
- 🏘️ **Village life doesn't require huge capital**
- 👥 **Community-based support network**
- 🛠️ **Hands-on skills = sustainable income**

### The Death: Same Pattern, Different Business

**Why this venture also failed:**

1. **Same "We Know Each Other" Exploitation**
   - 💬 **"We're villagemates, I'll pay next month"**
   - 📉 Never pays

2. **Medical Emergencies Drain Capital**
   - 🩺 Mary's health issues
   - 💊 Medication costs
   - 🏥 Hospital visits
   - **Every medical crisis = business capital gone**

3. **Unstable Village Economy**
   - 📉 Villagers themselves are poor—can't consistently buy
   - 🌧️ **Drought years = no income for anyone**
   - 💸 **No cash flow = business dies**

**Final Outcome:**
> **Baking/detergent/soap business dies. But Mary STILL doesn't give up.**

## 🏙️ 2012-2020: Nairobi Hustle—City Survival

### The Move: Trying the Capital City

**2012:**
- 🚌 **Mary moves to Nairobi** (Kenya's capital)
- 🎯 **Goal:** Find "manageable hustles" to support herself and her 2 school-going kids
- 🏢 **City = More opportunities** (she hopes)

**What she did in Nairobi:**
- 🛒 **Informal sector work** (selling goods, small-scale trading)
- 🧹 **Any work she could manage** with limited mobility
- 👧 **Supported by her firstborn daughter** (who acted as caregiver)

**Who was left behind:**
- 🏡 **Lydia stayed in the village** (with Mary's mother as caregiver)
- 👧 **The orphaned girl (Mary's sister's child)** also in the village

### 2019: The Poisoning—Everything Falls Apart

**Refer to [Mary & Lydia's Story](/blog/mary-lydia-story-20-years-resilience-disability-kenya):**
- ☠️ **2 family members poisoned, Mary's mother (the caregiver), Mary's sister**
- 😭 **Both Mary's mother & Mary's sister dies, 2 weeks apart, same hospital**
- 🏚️ **Housing crisis intensify** (threats of land grabbing)
- 💔 **Lydia now has no caregiver**

**Impact on Mary's Nairobi life:**
- 🚫 **Cannot stay in Nairobi without a caregiver**
- 💸 **Hiring a caregiver in Nairobi = UNAFFORDABLE**
- 👧 **Lydia and the orphaned girl need care too**
- 🧮 **Math doesn't work:** Mary needs a caregiver + Lydia needs a caregiver + child needs care = IMPOSSIBLE

### 2020: COVID-19—Forced Return to Village

**March 2020:**
- 🦠 **COVID-19 lockdowns**
- 🏙️ **Nairobi becomes UNBEARABLE** (no work, no income, high cost of living)
- 🚌 **Mary returns to the village**
- 🏡 **Mary + Lydia reunite in the village** (cheaper to survive together)
- 👧 **Firstborn daughter stays in Nairobi to hustle**

**But Nairobi isn't kind to the daughter either:**
- 💼 **Multiple sudden layoffs**
- 📉 **Unstable gig economy**
- 😰 **She's struggling too**

**Why we focus on Mary, Lydia, and the child:**
> They are the most urgent in need. The daughter is trying to survive herself.

**Final Outcome:**
> **Mary's 8-year Nairobi hustle ends. Back to village poverty. But she's STILL fighting.**

## 🏦 Lydia's Story: M-PESA Agent & Barbershop Receptionist

### Education Denied: The Exam Registration Scandal

**Lydia's background:**
- 🎓 **Studied under Kenya's 8-4-4 education system**
- 📚 **Completed 8 years of primary school**
- 🧠 **Smart, capable, eager to learn**

**But:**
- 🚫 **The school REFUSED to register her for national exams (KCPE)**
- 💬 **School's reason:** _"We don't want bad grades. You'll tarnish our reputation."_
- 📊 **Schools are ranked by exam scores**—disabled students are seen as "liabilities"

**Result:**
- 🎓 **No primary school certificate**
- 🚫 **Cannot pursue secondary education or vocational training** (certificate required)
- 💔 **Lydia becomes a DROPOUT—not by choice, but by discrimination**

**If you think this is rare in Kenya:**
> This is systematic. Disabled students are routinely denied exam registration to protect school rankings.

### No Tech Access = No Online Opportunities

**Why Lydia stayed stuck:**
- 🌐 **No internet in the village** (until now, we're raising funds for Starlink)
- 💻 **No computer or smartphone**
- 📚 **No access to online courses, remote work, tech skills**

**If she had tech access in 2010:**
- 👩‍💻 She could have learned coding, design, virtual assistance
- 🌍 She could have worked remotely for global clients
- 💰 She could have earned USD/EUR income from Kenya

**But without access:**
> She was trapped in the village with zero opportunities.

### The Businesses: M-PESA Agent & Barbershop Receptionist

**What Lydia did:**

#### 1. **M-PESA Mobile Money Agent**
- 📱 **M-PESA = Kenya's mobile money system** (send/receive money via phone)
- 🏪 **Lydia ran a small M-PESA agent kiosk**
- 💵 **Villagers come to deposit, withdraw, send money**
- 🧍‍♀️ **Perfect for Lydia:** Standing job, no need to walk around

**Why it worked (briefly):**
- 📍 **She stood at the kiosk all day** (immobility not a barrier)
- 💰 **Earned commission on transactions**
- 👥 **Helped the community with financial services**

#### 2. **Kinyozi (Barbershop) Receptionist**
- ✂️ **Kinyozi = Local barbershop**
- 🧍‍♀️ **Lydia stood at reception**, took payments, managed appointments
- 💇 **Again, standing job suited her limitations**

### Why Both Businesses Collapsed

**1. Medical Emergencies "Eat" All Capital**
- 🩹 **Lydia's 10+ year untreated leg wounds** flare up
- 🏥 **Emergency hospital visits**
- 💊 **Medication costs**
- 💸 **All business capital goes to medical bills**

**The Cycle:**
1. ✅ Build business capital ($50-100)
2. 🩺 Medical emergency hits
3. 💸 Capital = $0
4. 🔄 Take loan, crowdfund, start from ZERO
5. 📉 Repeat forever (rat race never ends)

**2. "Living Healthy Is a Blessing"**
- 🏥 **Disability = Unpredictable medical costs**
- 💰 **No insurance, no savings**
- 🚑 **Every emergency = bankruptcy**

**3. Unstable Village Economy**
- 📉 **Villagers themselves are poor**
- 💸 **Low transaction volumes** (M-PESA commissions = pennies)
- 🌧️ **Drought years = zero business**

**4. Poor Management? Or Impossible Circumstances?**
> **"Maybe someone can say this lacks good management for the business but be in their 'shoes'"**

**From their perspective:**
- 🏚️ **Surviving in a collapsing house** (housing crisis ongoing)
- 🩺 **Medical emergencies every few months**
- 💸 **No safety net, no insurance**
- 🌍 **No access to global markets** (no internet until now)

> **It's NOT poor management. It's IMPOSSIBLE circumstances.**

**Final Outcome:**
> **M-PESA business: Dead. Barbershop job: Gone. But Lydia still has hope.**
