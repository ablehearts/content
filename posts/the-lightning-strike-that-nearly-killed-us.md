## ⛈️ The Storm That Should Have Warned Us

**2017.** The year that taught us nature doesn't negotiate with the vulnerable.

It started like many storms in rural Kenya - dark clouds gathering, thunder rumbling in the distance. But this storm was different. **It thundered without rain.**

We should have known something was wrong.

## 🌲 The Tree Everyone Wanted

On our property stood a magnificent tree - tall, strong, valuable. The kind of tree that wealthy people covet. The kind that makes neighbors jealous. The kind that whispers money to those who don't own it.

**Everyone wanted that tree.**

- Neighbors asked to buy it
- Timber merchants made offers
- People suggested we cut it down and sell the wood
- Even family members hinted we should monetize it

We refused. The tree was ours. It provided:
- Shade in brutal heat
- A landmark for our small plot
- Natural beauty in our struggling existence
- Pride in ownership

**We didn't know it would become a lightning rod - literally.**

## ⚡ The Day Heaven Struck Earth

### 🐄 The Setup

It was an ordinary day that turned extraordinary in seconds. The compound:
- **Mary's mother (Grandma)** - outside, bringing the cattle home before the storm
- **Lydia** - at home, disabled, near the grass-thatched house
- **The cattle** - being herded by Grandma
- **Mary** - NOT there (I was in Nairobi hustling, as covered in our entrepreneurial journey story)
- **Lydia's sister** - NOT there (Nairobi schooling + hustling)

**Just Grandma, Lydia, and the cattle. That's who faced the lightning.**

### 🌧️ The Storm Without Rain

The clouds came. Thunder roared. But **no rain fell**.

In rural Kenya, we know storms. Rain brings life - it waters crops, fills wells, cools the air. Thunder without rain is unusual. Thunder without rain is ominous.

### 🐄 Grandma Brings the Cattle Home

Mary's mother saw the signs - storm approaching, getting late, cattle still out. She had no one to help her, so she went herself to bring them to the compound before the storm hit.

**She didn't make it back in time.**

## ⚡ The Strike

### 💥 A Moment That Changed Everything

The lightning didn't strike nearby. It didn't hit the ground gently. **It struck the very tall tree with devastating force.**

The tree everyone wanted. The tree we protected. The tree that stood as the tallest around at a prime junction.

**The lightning shattered it into small pieces.**

But it didn't stop there.

### 👵🐄 Midst of Cattle and Grandma

**The lightning struck midst of the cattle and Mary's mother.**

- Mary's mother (Grandma) was **knocked unconscious instantly**
- One of the cows **ran mad** for some time from the electrical shock
- The chaos was immediate, violent, terrifying

In rural Kenya, when someone is struck by lightning and falls:
- There's no 911 to call
- No ambulance arriving in minutes
- No emergency room around the corner
- No paramedics with defibrillators

**There's just panic, neighbors, and prayer.**

### 😢 Lydia's Face

At the same moment, at the door of the grass-thatched house, **Lydia was struck**.

She **fell down face first** from the lightning's force.

The impact distorted her face:
- Facial trauma from the fall
- Tissue damage
- Disfigurement adding to her existing disability
- Permanent reminder of that terrifying day

**She was already disabled. Now she was scarred.**

### 🏘️ The Villagers Respond

Why did help come quickly? **The tree.**

The very tall tree - the tallest around - fell at a **prime junction**. The loud crash, the location, the visibility - it created immediate attention.

Villagers rushed to the scene:
- Found Grandma unconscious
- Found Lydia injured and traumatized
- Witnessed the mad cow running
- Saw the tree shattered into pieces

They provided **basic medication** and support. Then they did all they could: **let God care for the recovery.**

## 🏥 The Aftermath: Basic Care, Divine Recovery

### 🆘 What They Needed:

- **Immediate medical care** - Lightning strike injuries need specialized treatment
- **Psychological support** - Trauma counseling for near-death survivors
- **Scar treatment** - Especially for Lydia's facial distortion
- **Recovery time** - Rest and proper nutrition to heal

### ✝️ What They Got:

- **Basic medication from villagers**
- **Prayer**
- **Faith that God would handle the rest**

No hospital admission because they couldn't afford it. No advanced treatment because it wasn't accessible in rural areas. No counseling because it doesn't exist for poor disabled families.

**They recovered by God's grace, and by basic medical intervention.**

Grandma regained consciousness, but she was never the same. The electrical shock aged her instantly. Her strength - already diminished by poverty and age - never returned. (She has since passed away - the [poisoning in 2019](/blog/mary-lydia-story-20-years-resilience-disability-kenya)])

Lydia's facial distortion became a permanent addition to her existing disability challenges.

**Every day she sees the lightning strike's impact in the mirror.**

## 🌳 The Tree's Fate: Firewood for the Tea Industry

Here's what happened to the tree that nearly killed them:

**The tree was shattered into small pieces by the lightning strike.**

What remained:
- No longer majestic
- Already "valueless" from the destruction
- **Culturally forbidden to use** after the bad incident

**The tea industry nearby acquired it as cheap firewood.**

Even in destruction, the tree that everyone wanted served commercial interests - but brought no benefit to the family that owned it, suffered from it, and nearly died because of it.

## 💔 Lydia's New Layer of Pain

### ⏮️ Before the Lightning Strike:

Lydia was already struggling with:
- Progressive disability
- Mobility limitations
- Body image issues
- Social isolation
- Rejection from father's family

### ⏭️ After the Lightning Strike:

Add to that list:
- **Facial distortion** - visible, permanent, from the fall
- **Lightning strike survivor trauma** - PTSD from near-death experience
- **Deepened insecurity** - "Now I'm disabled AND scarred from lightning"
- **Additional social stigma** - Some believe lightning strikes are curses or divine punishment

**How much can one young woman endure?**

## ❓ The Questions Nobody Asks

### 🏥 "Why didn't they go to the hospital?"

**With what money?** The family was already struggling to eat. Hospital care for lightning strike victims costs money they don't have.

They chose survival over medical bills. They chose basic village medication and prayer over debt. **They chose to stay poor but alive rather than die in debt.**

### 💰 "Did they get insurance payouts?"

What insurance? Poor disabled families in rural Kenya don't have:
- Home insurance
- Health insurance
- Life insurance
- Lightning strike coverage

**Insurance is a luxury for people who have something to insure.**

### 🏛️ "Couldn't the government help?"

The Kenyan government offers minimal assistance to disabled citizens. For lightning strike victims who are already disabled and already poor? **They don't even register on the priority list.**

### ⚖️ "Why didn't they sue someone?"

Sue whom? Nature? God? The tree? **You can't sue poverty into compensation.**

## 🩹 Living With the Scars

### 🤕 Physical Scars:

- Lydia's facial distortion - permanent, visible, daily reminder
- Grandma's diminished strength - she aged overnight, never recovered (now deceased)
- The mad cow - traumatized animal that survived

### 🧠 Psychological Scars:

- **Fear of storms** - Every thunder now triggers panic
- **Survivor's guilt** - Why did they survive when they could have easily died?
- **Trauma triggers** - Lightning, thunder, even tall trees cause anxiety
- **Helplessness** - The realization that they survived by luck and divine intervention, not by preparedness

### 👥 Social Scars:

- **Increased stigma** - "The family that was struck by lightning"
- **Superstitious neighbors** - Some avoid them, believing they're cursed
- **Lost opportunities** - Lydia's facial scars add another barrier to social acceptance
- **Isolation** - Even more cut off from normal community life

## 📚 What We Learned

### 1️⃣ Disasters Don't Discriminate - But Recovery Does

The lightning didn't care that:
- Grandma was just trying to protect the cattle
- Lydia was already disabled
- The family was already poor
- They were already marginalized

But **recovery resources do discriminate.** Wealthy families get:
- Immediate medical care
- Plastic surgery for scars
- Psychological counseling
- Insurance payouts
- Time to heal without financial pressure

**They got none of that.**

### 2️⃣ Visible Scars Change Everything

Lydia's progressive disability was somewhat manageable. But **facial distortion from lightning is always visible.**

Every interaction now includes:
- Stares at her face
- Questions about "what happened"
- Assumptions about her character based on appearance
- Another layer of "different" in a world that punishes difference

### 3️⃣ Trauma Compounds

Lightning strike survivors often develop:
- PTSD
- Anxiety disorders
- Depression
- Fear of storms

Add that to:
- Existing disability challenges
- Poverty-related stress
- Social rejection
- Family abandonment
- Mother (Mary) being away in Nairobi hustling for survival

**How much trauma can one person carry before they break?**

## 🌳 The Tree's Legacy

The tree that everyone wanted:
- Shattered by lightning
- Became cheap firewood for tea industry
- Culturally forbidden to use after the tragedy
- Brought no benefit to the family who owned it

**The tree that nearly killed them served commercial interests in death, but brought the family only suffering.**

Some days we're grateful it's gone - one less reminder of that day. Other days we wonder if selling it earlier would have prevented the tragedy. But how could we have known?

## 📅 Seven Years Later

It's been years since 2017. The physical scars remain:
- Lydia still sees the lightning strike's impact in her mirror every morning
- Grandma never regained her full strength (she passed away)
- The memory of the mad cow running haunts them

The psychological scars are deeper:
- Every storm triggers panic
- They count the seconds between lightning and thunder
- They avoid standing near tall trees during storms
- They live with the knowledge that survival was God's grace, not their preparation

## 💡 Why This Story Matters

Most people donate to help with:
- Wheelchairs
- Medical care
- Food
- Education

But disasters don't stop because you're already struggling. **They need help not just with ongoing disabilities, but with disaster recovery they couldn't afford years ago.**

### 🔄 What Could Have Been Different With Resources:

**Immediate aftermath:**
- Ambulance transport to hospital
- Emergency care for lightning strike victims
- Professional medical treatment
- Pain management

**Long-term recovery:**
- Reconstructive treatment for Lydia's facial distortion
- Psychological counseling for all victims
- PTSD therapy
- Proper trauma care for Grandma (might have prolonged her life)

**Prevention for future:**
- Lightning rod installation
- Safe storm shelter
- Emergency medical fund
- Weather warning systems

**They got none of this because they had no money.**

## ⚡ The Lightning Strike Isn't Over

The physical strike happened in 2017. But the **impact continues** every single day:

- Lydia wakes up with facial distortion reminding her of that day
- They panic during every storm
- Grandma's decline (before she passed) was accelerated by the strike
- They remain unable to afford proper reconstructive treatment
- The trauma of seeing Grandma unconscious and the cow running mad haunts them

**The lightning struck once. The trauma strikes daily.**

## 📣 Our Message

If you're reading this thinking, "That could never happen to me" - you're probably right. Because you likely have:
- Insurance
- Access to emergency services
- Medical care
- Resources to recover
- A safety net

**They had none of that. They still don't.**

When you help us, you're not just addressing current needs. **You're helping them recover from a disaster they survived by God's grace but couldn't afford to properly treat.**
