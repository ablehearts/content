## 🌳 Mary's Story: When the Banana Tree Changed Everything

### 💥 The Accident

I was young, full of life and dreams. Then came the day that divided my life into "before" and "after."

A banana tree accident. That's all it takes sometimes - one moment, and your entire future changes trajectory.

The injury left me disabled. My legs, which once carried me freely through our village, now needed support. Walking became a challenge. Independence became complicated.

But life had more in store for me.

## 👶 Against All Odds: Motherhood

### 👭 Two Daughters

Despite my disability, I became pregnant. Not once, but twice.

Many people in our community couldn't understand it. How could a disabled woman have children? The whispers started even before my daughters were born.

But I gave birth to two beautiful daughters:
- **Lydia** - my secondborn, who would later develop her own disabilities
- Her elder sister - who is able-bodied

### 🚫 The In-Laws' Rejection

When my daughters were born, I thought their father's family would embrace us. Instead, I faced something worse than physical disability: **complete rejection**.

The in-laws wanted nothing to do with me or my children. In their eyes:
- I was disabled, therefore "less than"
- My children were born to a disabled woman - a mark against them
- We were not worthy of the family name

Their father's family turned their backs on us. No support. No acknowledgment. No mercy.

**Two innocent children and their disabled mother - abandoned.**

## ❓ Lydia's Mysterious Journey

### 🏃‍♀️ A Healthy Start

Lydia was born able-bodied. For her early years, she ran, played, and moved like any other child. We had hope that at least she would be spared what I endured.

Then something changed.

### 🦵 The Progressive Disability

Around age 10-11, Lydia began experiencing changes no doctor could fully explain:

- Her legs started weakening
- Walking became increasingly difficult
- Her spine began developing issues
- Progressive physical limitations set in

**We don't know why.**

Was it genetic? Environmental? A delayed manifestation of something present from birth? Medical care in rural Kenya is scarce, and specialists are expensive. We may never have answers.

What we know is this: **My daughter, who once ran freely, now struggles with mobility just like her mother.**

## ⚖️ The Weight of Two Disabilities

### 👩‍👧 Mother and Daughter

Now we face the world together - two disabled women trying to survive in a society that already marginalizes the able-bodied poor.

**Our daily reality:**
- Neither of us can work traditional manual labor jobs
- We can't farm effectively - the primary income source in rural Kenya
- We can't travel easily for opportunities
- We depend on each other, but both of us need help
- Lydia's younger sister helps, but she has her own life to build

### 🔇 The In-Laws' Continued Silence

Even after Lydia developed disabilities - even as they watched their granddaughter struggle - the in-laws maintained their rejection.

No offers of help. No medical support. No acknowledgment that this child is part of their bloodline.

**Disability, it seems, is an unforgivable sin in their eyes.**

## 💕 Romance, Body Image, and Disability

### 🤫 The Unspoken Struggle

Let's talk about what nobody discusses: **disabled women and romantic relationships**.

The world assumes we don't have:
- Romantic desires
- Physical needs
- Dreams of partnership
- Rights to intimacy

### 👩 Mary's Experience

I had relationships. I had children. But romance when you're disabled is complicated by:

**Physical challenges:**
- Can't move easily or freely
- Dependence on others for basic mobility
- Constant pain and fatigue
- Limited ability to engage in "normal" courtship activities

**Emotional burdens:**
- Feeling like a burden to any partner
- Constant fear of abandonment (which eventually happened)
- Society's judgment: "Why would anyone want a disabled woman?"
- Family pressure and rejection

**Body image:**
- My legs don't work like others'
- I can't dress fashionably in ways that require standing/walking
- Traditional beauty standards exclude disabled bodies
- Constant comparison to able-bodied women

### 👧 Lydia's Current Struggle

Lydia is now a young woman. She has:
- Dreams of romance
- Desires for normal teenage/young adult experiences
- Hope for future relationships

But she also faces:

**Deepening insecurity:**
- "Why would anyone want me when I can't even walk properly?"
- "My own father's family rejected me - why would strangers accept me?"
- "I'm disabled like my mother - will I face the same rejection?"

**Limited social opportunities:**
- Can't go to social events easily
- Can't participate in community activities
- Physical limitations restrict dating options
- Transportation challenges isolate her further

**The lightning strike scars:**
- Facial disfigurement from the 2017 lightning strike (more on this in another post)
- Added another layer to body image struggles
- "Now I'm disabled AND scarred"

## ❔ The Questions Nobody Asks

### For Mary:

**"How did you have children while disabled?"**

The same way any woman does. My reproductive system works. My legs don't. People conflate the two as if disability makes you less human, less sexual, less deserving of family.

**"Why did their father leave?"**

Because society taught him that a disabled woman is temporary, not someone you build a life with. Because his family rejected us. Because he could find an able-bodied woman with "less complications."

**"Don't you regret having children?"**

Never. Not for one second. My daughters are my purpose. Even with all our struggles, they are the reason I fight every day.

### For Lydia:

**"How does it feel watching your mother struggle, knowing you might face the same future?"**

Terrifying. I see my mother's exhaustion, her pain, her constant battles. And I think: "Is this my future? Will I also be rejected? Will I also be alone?"

**"Do you blame your mother for your disability?"**

Never. Whatever caused my condition, it wasn't her fault. She's my hero, not my villain. We face this together.

**"Do you think you'll find love?"**

(Lydia's answer) "I want to. But I'm scared. I've watched how society treats disabled women. I've felt the rejection from my own family. Why would a stranger see value in me when my own blood doesn't?"

## 🎭 The Body Image Battle

### 👁️ What Society Sees:

- Broken bodies
- Burdens
- Objects of pity
- Incomplete humans
- Asexual beings
- Economic liabilities

### ✨ What We Actually Are:

- **Whole people** with complex emotions
- Women with dreams, desires, and dignity
- Survivors of extraordinary circumstances
- Fighters who wake up every day despite the odds
- Mothers, daughters, humans - not disabilities with bodies attached

## 🏝️ The Isolation of Disability + Poverty

In wealthy countries, disabled people have:
- Dating apps with accessibility features
- Adaptive equipment that enables independence
- Disability communities and support groups
- Legal protections against discrimination
- Medical care and pain management

In rural Kenya, disabled women have:
- Social isolation
- Limited mobility aids
- No community support systems
- No legal protections
- Constant physical pain with minimal relief
- **Zero romantic prospects because we're seen as liabilities**

## 📢 Our Message to the World

### 👋 To Able-Bodied People:

Stop assuming disabled people don't have:
- Sexual desires
- Romantic needs
- Body image concerns
- Rights to partnership and intimacy

We're not asexual. We're not undesirable. We're not "broken" in ways that matter to love.

**Disability affects our mobility, not our humanity.**

### 💪 To Disabled Women Everywhere:

You are not alone in feeling:
- Undesirable
- Like a burden
- Afraid of rejection
- Frustrated by your body
- Angry at the limitations

But also know:
- **Your worth is not determined by your mobility**
- Your body tells a story of survival
- The right person will see your whole self, not just your disability
- You deserve love, respect, and dignity - not charity or pity

### 💝 To Potential Donors:

When you help us with:
- Wheelchairs and mobility aids
- Better housing
- Medical care
- Financial stability

You're not just addressing physical needs. **You're restoring dignity. You're enabling social participation. You're giving us the independence needed to build relationships from a position of strength, not desperation.**

## 📖 The Unfinished Story

This isn't a story with a happy ending - yet. We don't have:
- Romantic partners who stayed
- Financial security
- Full mobility
- Social acceptance
- Family support

But we have:
- Each other
- Hope
- Fight
- Stories worth telling
- Dreams still worth pursuing

**And maybe, with your help, we can write a better next chapter.**
