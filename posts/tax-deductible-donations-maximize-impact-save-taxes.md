## 💰 Understanding Tax-Deductible Donations

When you give to a qualified (registered, tax-exempt) charity, the tax system in many countries lets you deduct some or all of that gift. Many donors don't fully understand how these deductions work.

**Where AbleHearts stands:** AbleHearts is not yet a registered charity and does not have tax-exempt status, so gifts to AbleHearts are **not** tax-deductible today. Everything below describes how deductions work for gifts to organizations that *do* qualify, so you can plan your giving with clear eyes.

This guide explains how US tax-deductible donations work, what the limits are, and what records the IRS expects.

---

## 📋 What Makes a Donation Tax-Deductible?

### ✅ Qualifying Requirements

For your donation to be tax-deductible, it must meet these IRS criteria:

1. **Donate to a Qualified Organization**
   - 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization
   - Religious organizations (churches, temples, mosques)
   - Government entities for public purposes
   - Verified on IRS Tax Exempt Organization Search

2. **You Must Itemize Deductions**
   - Cannot claim if taking standard deduction
   - Standard deduction 2025: $14,600 (single), $29,200 (married)
   - Must have charitable + other deductions exceeding standard amount

3. **You Must Have Documentation**
   - Receipt for all donations
   - Bank statement or written acknowledgment for $250+
   - Detailed records for non-cash donations

### ❌ What's NOT Tax-Deductible

- Donations to individuals (even if they need help)
- Political contributions
- Donations to foreign charities (without US 501(c)(3) partnership)
- Value of your time/service volunteering
- Raffle tickets, bingo, lottery (entertainment portion)

## 💵 How Much Can You Deduct?

### Annual Deduction Limits (2025)

The IRS limits how much you can deduct based on your **Adjusted Gross Income (AGI)**:

#### Cash Donations:
- **Up to 60% of AGI** to qualified public charities
- **Up to 30% of AGI** to private foundations

#### Non-Cash Donations:
- **Up to 50% of AGI** for property (held >1 year)
- **Up to 30% of AGI** for appreciated property

### 📊 Real Examples:

**Example 1: Single Filer**
- AGI: $80,000
- Maximum cash deduction: $48,000 (60% of $80,000)
- You donate: $5,000 to a qualified US charity
- ✅ Fully deductible

**Example 2: Married Filing Jointly**
- AGI: $150,000
- Maximum cash deduction: $90,000 (60% of $150,000)
- You donate: $10,000 to a qualified US charity
- ✅ Fully deductible

**Example 3: High Earner**
- AGI: $500,000
- Maximum cash deduction: $300,000 (60% of $500,000)
- You donate: $50,000 to a qualified US charity
- ✅ Fully deductible

## 🧮 Calculate Your Tax Savings

### How Tax Deductions Save You Money

**Tax deductions reduce your taxable income**, which means you pay less in taxes. The amount you save depends on your **tax bracket**.

### 2025 Federal Tax Brackets:

### 💡 Quick Calculation Formula:

**Tax Savings = Donation Amount × Your Tax Bracket**

**Example Calculations:**

**Scenario 1: $500 Donation, 22% Tax Bracket**
- Tax savings: $500 × 0.22 = **$110**
- Net cost after tax savings: $500 - $110 = **$390**

**Scenario 2: $2,000 Donation, 32% Tax Bracket**
- Tax savings: $2,000 × 0.32 = **$640**
- Net cost: $2,000 - $640 = **$1,360**

**Scenario 3: $10,000 Donation, 37% Tax Bracket**
- Tax savings: $10,000 × 0.37 = **$3,700**
- Net cost: $10,000 - $3,700 = **$6,300**

## 📝 Documentation Requirements

### What You Need to Keep

The IRS has specific documentation requirements based on donation amount:

#### Donations Under $250:
- Bank statement showing:
  - Name of charity
  - Date of donation
  - Amount donated
- OR receipt from charity

#### Donations $250 - $500:
- Written acknowledgment from charity stating:
  - Amount of cash donation
  - Description of non-cash donation
  - Statement that no goods/services were provided
  - OR description and value of goods/services if provided

#### Donations $501 - $5,000:
- All documentation from $250+ category
- PLUS your own records showing:
  - How you acquired the property
  - Date of acquisition
  - Cost or other basis
  - Fair market value determination method

#### Donations Over $5,000:
- All documentation from $501+ category
- PLUS qualified appraisal for non-cash items
- Form 8283 (Noncash Charitable Contributions)

### 🚨 Common Documentation Mistakes:

❌ **"I donated cash at church, but I don't have a receipt"**
- Solution: Always get a receipt or use check/credit card for records

❌ **"I donated $500, but I only have an email confirmation"**
- Solution: That's fine! Email counts as written acknowledgment

❌ **"I donated clothes worth $1,000, but I didn't get an appraisal"**
- Solution: For $1,000, appraisal not needed. Keep receipt + list of items

## 🎯 Strategies to Maximize Your Deduction

### 1. Bunch Your Donations 📦

**The Problem:** Your annual donations don't exceed the standard deduction.

**The Solution:** "Bunch" 2-3 years of donations into one year.

**Example:**
- Standard deduction: $14,600 (single)
- Normal giving: $5,000/year

**Without Bunching (3 years):**
- Year 1: Donate $5,000, take standard deduction ($14,600)
- Year 2: Donate $5,000, take standard deduction ($14,600)
- Year 3: Donate $5,000, take standard deduction ($14,600)
- Total deductions: $43,800 (standard × 3)
- Total given: $15,000

**With Bunching (3 years):**
- Year 1: Donate $15,000, itemize ($15,000 + $2,000 other = $17,000)
- Year 2: Donate $0, take standard deduction ($14,600)
- Year 3: Donate $0, take standard deduction ($14,600)
- Total deductions: $46,200 (itemize + standard × 2)
- Total given: $15,000
- **Extra tax savings: $2,400 × your tax rate**

### 2. Donate Appreciated Stock 📈

**Why It's Powerful:**
- Avoid capital gains tax on appreciation
- Deduct full market value
- Double tax benefit!

**Example:**
You bought stock for $1,000, now worth $5,000.

**If You Sell Stock Then Donate Cash:**
- Capital gains tax: $4,000 × 15% = **$600 tax paid**
- Donate $4,400 after-tax proceeds
- Deduction: $4,400

**If You Donate Stock Directly:**
- Capital gains tax: **$0** (avoided entirely)
- Donate full $5,000 worth
- Deduction: $5,000
- **You saved $600 + helped more!**

### 3. Use a Donor-Advised Fund (DAF) 🏦

**What It Is:** A charitable investment account.

**How It Works:**
1. Donate money/stock to DAF (get immediate tax deduction)
2. Funds grow tax-free in the account
3. Recommend grants to qualified charities over time

**Benefits:**
- Take deduction this year, give later
- Simplify recordkeeping (1 receipt vs. many)
- Donate anonymously if desired
- Grow your charitable impact tax-free

### 4. Make a Qualified Charitable Distribution (QCD) 👴

**Who Can Use This:** Age 70½ or older with an IRA

**How It Works:**
- Transfer up to $105,000 (2025 limit) from your IRA directly to charity
- Counts toward your Required Minimum Distribution (RMD)
- **NOT included in your taxable income** (even better than a deduction!)

**Example:**
- Age 72, RMD required: $15,000
- Transfer $15,000 from IRA to a qualified charity as a QCD
- You satisfy RMD requirement
- $15,000 never appears as income
- You save: $15,000 × your tax rate

### 5. Time Your Donations Strategically 📅

**End-of-Year Giving:**
- Donate by December 31 to claim deduction for that year
- Credit card donations count when charged (even if paid later)
- Check/cash must be delivered by Dec 31

**High-Income Year:**
- Got a bonus? Donate more this year to offset higher income
- Sold property at a gain? Donate to reduce taxable income

## 🧾 How to Claim Your Deduction (Step-by-Step)

### Step 1: Gather Your Documentation
- Collect all donation receipts
- Organize by charity and date
- Total your contributions

### Step 2: Determine If You Should Itemize
- Add up all itemized deductions:
  - Charitable contributions
  - State/local taxes (capped at $10,000)
  - Mortgage interest
  - Medical expenses exceeding 7.5% of AGI
- If total > standard deduction → itemize
- If total < standard deduction → take standard

### Step 3: Complete IRS Forms
- **Schedule A (Form 1040):** Report itemized deductions
- **Line 11:** Cash contributions
- **Line 12:** Non-cash contributions
- **Form 8283:** If non-cash donations > $500

### Step 4: Keep Records for 3+ Years
- IRS can audit within 3 years
- Keep donation receipts, bank statements
- Store electronically for easy access

## ❓ FAQs: Tax-Deductible Donations

### Q: Can I deduct donations if I take the standard deduction?
**A:** No. You must itemize deductions to claim charitable contributions. However, there was a special provision during COVID that expired in 2021.

### Q: I donated $500 but charity only sent a $400 receipt. What do I do?
**A:** Only deduct the $400 with proper documentation. Contact charity to correct the receipt if it's an error.

### Q: Can I deduct donations to Mary & Lydia, or to AbleHearts?
**A:** No. Donations to individuals aren't tax-deductible, and AbleHearts is not yet a registered charity or a qualified 501(c)(3) organization, so gifts to AbleHearts aren't tax-deductible either. We will update this post once our registration is complete.

### Q: What if my donation is over the AGI limit?
**A:** You can carry forward the excess for up to 5 years. For example, if your AGI is $100,000 and you donate $70,000, you can deduct $60,000 this year and $10,000 next year.

### Q: Do I need an appraisal for donated items?
**A:** Only for non-cash items valued over $5,000. For example, donating a car worth $6,000 requires an appraisal.

### Q: Can I deduct the value of my volunteer time?
**A:** No. However, you CAN deduct unreimbursed expenses related to volunteering (e.g., mileage at 14¢/mile, supplies purchased).

### Q: What if I get something in return for my donation?
**A:** You can only deduct the amount that exceeds the fair market value of what you received. For example, if you pay $100 for a charity dinner worth $40, you can deduct $60.
