# Women's Organizations: Support Menstrual Dignity for Disabled Women

## 🚺 The Hidden Crisis: Period Poverty Meets Disability

**Imagine being Mary—wheelchair-bound, unable to work, and facing menstruation with nothing but old rags.**

Every month, **millions of disabled women** in Kenya face an impossible choice:
- 🩸 Use dirty cloth scraps that cause infections
- 🩸 Stay isolated at home to avoid public shame
- 🩸 Skip meals to buy a single pack of pads

**This is not a hygiene issue. This is a DIGNITY crisis.**

For able-bodied women, period poverty is a barrier. For **women with disabilities**, it's a **prison sentence**—compounding isolation, infection risk, and dehumanization.

---

## 💔 Mary, Lydia & the Orphan's Reality

**Three women. Three stories. One shared struggle.**

### Mary (Disabled mother)
- **Monthly income:** $0
- **Sanitary pad access:** Relies on charitable donations (unreliable)
- **Reality:** Uses old t-shirts torn into strips, washed and reused until threadbare
- **Infection history:** 3 UTIs in 2024 from unhygienic materials
- **Shame:** Cannot leave home during menstruation (5-7 days/month = 60-84 days/year in isolation)

### Lydia (Disabled Since Childhood)
- **Monthly income:** $0
- **Sanitary pad access:** Borrows from neighbors (humiliating)
- **Reality:** Sometimes goes without underwear to preserve the few pairs she owns
- **Health impact:** Chronic yeast infections, reproductive health at risk
- **Mental health:** Depression from constant indignity

### The Orphan (Identity Protected, Teen)
- **Monthly income:** $0
- **Sanitary pad access:** None—school attendance drops 25% during menstruation
- **Reality:** Misses school 3-5 days/month, falling behind academically
- **Risk:** Vulnerable to exploitation by men offering money for pads
- **Future:** Education interrupted = poverty cycle continues

**These are not statistics. These are our sisters.**

## 🩸 What Menstrual Dignity Means for Disabled Women

**For able-bodied women:**
Period poverty = inconvenience, health risk, reduced mobility

**For women with disabilities:**
Period poverty = total social isolation + severe health risks + loss of human dignity

### Why It's Worse for Disabled Women:

**1. Limited Mobility**
- Cannot easily access shops or public restrooms
- Changing pads requires assistance (privacy violation)
- Wheelchair users risk pressure sores from sitting in soiled pads

**2. Income Inequality**
- **98% unemployment rate** for disabled women in Kenya
- No money = no pads, no underwear, no choice

**3. Caregiver Dependency**
- Rely on others for pad changes (often male caregivers = shame)
- Abuse risk increases when caregivers control access to hygiene products

**4. Health Complications**
- Higher infection risk (limited mobility = delayed hygiene)
- Reproductive health neglect (can't afford gynecologist visits)
- Mental health: depression, PTSD from repeated indignity

**5. Social Exclusion**
- Already isolated due to disability stigma
- Period poverty = additional 60-84 days/year of forced isolation
- Community assumes "uncleanliness" = further marginalization

## 🌸 What Women's Organizations Can Provide

### 1. **Sanitary Pads (Reusable & Disposable)**

**Immediate Need:**
- **Disposable pads:** 20 pads/month × 12 months = 240 pads/year
- **Cost:** $3-5/month (affordable for organizations, impossible for Mary/Lydia)

**Sustainable Solution:**
- **Reusable cloth pads:** $15-20 one-time cost = 2-3 years of use
- **Menstrual cups:** $25 one-time cost = 10 years of use
- **Period underwear:** $30-40 = washable, discreet, eco-friendly

**Organizations that provide these:**
- **Days for Girls** (reusable pad kits)
- **ZanaAfrica** (pads + health education)
- **Pads4Girls Kenya** (school + community distribution)

**How to Donate:**
- Bulk purchase from manufacturers (cheaper per unit)
- Partner with local NGOs for distribution
- Sponsor monthly subscriptions ($5/woman/month)

### 2. **Clean Underwear**

**Current Reality:**
Mary owns **2 pairs** of underwear. Lydia owns **3**. The orphan owns **1**.

**During menstruation:**
- Must wash, dry, and reuse immediately (unsanitary)
- No backup if underwear is stained or damaged
- Shame from wearing visibly old/damaged undergarments

**What's Needed:**
- **6-8 pairs** of clean, comfortable cotton underwear per woman
- **Period-proof underwear** (leak-resistant, washable)
- **Sizes:** Inclusive sizing (XS-3XL for different body types)

**Cost:**
- Cotton underwear: $2-5/pair × 6 = $12-30/woman
- Period underwear: $15-25/pair × 3 = $45-75/woman (lasts years)

**Organizations:**
- **Underwear for Hope** (distributes to vulnerable women)
- **ThirdLove/Thinx** (donate period underwear via partnerships)

### 3. **Hygiene Kits**

**What's Included:**
- Sanitary pads (1-month supply)
- Clean underwear (3 pairs)
- Soap (gentle, pH-balanced for intimate hygiene)
- Wet wipes (for on-the-go cleaning)
- Small towel/washcloth
- Pain relief (ibuprofen for cramps)
- Dignity bag (discreet pouch to carry supplies)

**Cost per Kit:** $25-35
**Impact:** 1 month of menstrual dignity

**Organizations:**
- **Days for Girls** (hygiene kits with education)
- **Femme International** (Kenya-based menstrual health NGO)

### 4. **Menstrual Health Education**

**Why It Matters:**
Many disabled women were **never taught** about menstruation due to:
- Caregiver neglect (assumed they wouldn't menstruate)
- Exclusion from school health classes
- Cultural stigma (disability + menstruation = double taboo)

**What Education Includes:**
- How to use pads, tampons, menstrual cups safely
- Hygiene practices to prevent infections
- Recognizing abnormal bleeding (health red flags)
- Reproductive health rights (access to gynecologists)
- Body autonomy (resisting caregiver abuse)

**Organizations:**
- **ZanaAfrica** (menstrual health curriculum)
- **Sauti Kenya** (disability + sexual health education)

### 5. **Accessible Sanitation Facilities**

**Problem:**
Public restrooms are **not wheelchair accessible** in Kenya.

**Solution Women's Orgs Can Fund:**
- Install ramps, grab bars in community toilets
- Build accessible private changing rooms
- Provide portable toilets for rural areas
- Partner with AbleHearts to build accessible facilities

**Cost:**
- Retrofit 1 toilet: $500-1,000
- Build new accessible toilet: $2,000-5,000
- Portable accessible toilet: $300-600
